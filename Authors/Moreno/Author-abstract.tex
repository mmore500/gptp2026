Here, we develop a framework to characterize runtime behavior of best-effort code and apply it to two case studies of best-effort computing approaches in digital evolution projects.
The first case study, applying best-effort strategies to CPU-based multiprocessing, tests scaling efficiency, runtime quality-of-service, and robustness of both to hardware anomalies.
The second case study explores digital evolution on the Cerebras Wafer-Scale Engine, testing best-effort strategies to track evolution under constraints in memory, bandwidth, and data fidelity.
Cataloging potential forms of best-effort relaxation and scopes in which they might be applied, we argue that digital evolution is uniquely positioned to lead exploration of post-deterministic HPC and, indeed, already has a rich history of doing so.
