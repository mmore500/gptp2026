\caption{
\textbf{Conceptual framework for best-effort computing.}
\footnotesize
Best-effort computing strategies may incorporate tolerance for a chosen combination of asynchronicity, data attrition (both message loss and memory eviction), and data corruption (panel \ref{moreno:fig:best-effort:types}).
While such strategies can improve fault-robustness, scaling efficiency, and memory efficiency, departing from a deterministic execution model sacrifices exact computational reproducibility, and introduces potential for greater variance and even systematic bias in computational outcomes (panel \ref{moreno:fig:best-effort:tradeoffs}).
In the context of scientific computing, a distinction can be often drawn between an underlying computation (e.g., a simulation kernel) and operations performed to observe and record its behavior (panel \ref{moreno:fig:best-effort:components}).
To manage trade-offs between computational efficiency and computational integrity, best-effort strategies may be restricted to one or the other, or extended to both.
In either case, projects incorporating best-effort strategies should consider including instrumentation to characterize the runtime behavior; panel \ref{moreno:fig:best-effort:qos} suggests several metrics that may be collected.
}
